Aircraft Systems & Integration

UBC Uncrewed Aircraft Systems

Since joining UBC Uncrewed Aircraft Systems in January 2026, I have contributed to several UAV projects, ranging from mechanical mechanisms for our mothership VTOL to maintenance and integration work on our competition multirotors.

This year, I am the primary member responsible for software, electrical, and payload integration on our main competition multirotor aircraft. I am also assisting with and advising on the main airframe design, helping bring together the mechanical and electrical systems required for the aircraft.

January 2026 – Present

01 — Aircraft Integration

Competition Multirotor

My primary responsibility this year is integrating the software, electrical systems, and payload into our main competition multirotor. This involves working across different subsystems and helping ensure that the hardware, wiring, and software can function together as part of a complete aircraft.

Alongside this work, I am assisting with and advising on the main airframe design. This gives me the opportunity to consider how the mechanical layout, component mounting, electrical systems, and payload requirements affect one another, rather than treating each subsystem independently.

02 — Mechanical Design

Mothership VTOL Mechanisms

Before taking on my current integration responsibilities, I worked on mechanical components for the mothership VTOL. One of my projects involved designing and fabricating a mechanism that allows a propeller assembly to turn. This required translating the intended movement into a physical mechanism that could be incorporated into the aircraft.

I also designed and created a mechanical switch magnet for the VTOL. These projects gave me experience taking a mechanical concept through CAD and into a fabricated component, while considering how the mechanism would function as part of a larger aircraft system.

03 — Aircraft Maintenance

Supporting Competition Aircraft

I have also helped maintain the previous year's competition multirotor. This gave me additional exposure to an existing aircraft and the practical work involved in keeping its systems and components ready for use.

Across these projects, I have worked on both individual mechanical components and the integration of larger aircraft systems. This combination has helped me develop a broader understanding of how mechanical design, electrical hardware, software, and payload requirements interact in a functional UAV.

Engineering Takeaways

Designing Beyond Individual Components

My work with UBC UAS has given me the opportunity to contribute to different stages of UAV development, from designing and fabricating mechanical mechanisms to integrating the systems needed for a competition aircraft. It has reinforced the importance of considering the aircraft as a complete system, where mechanical design, electrical hardware, software, and payload requirements all influence one another.
