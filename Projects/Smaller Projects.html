SIDE-PROJECT 01

Compact 2-Way Retracting Rail

In Progress

Mechanical Design · Prototyping · Design Challenge

Project Description & Goals

Having always loved the Assassin's Creed game franchise, I have wondered since I was little how their iconic Hidden Blade — a compact, spring-loaded mechanism worn below the wrist — could extend and retract so quickly while remaining reliable and compact. As such, I decided to take on designing a non-functional-blade mock-up as a mechanical design challenge, focusing on the mechanism itself rather than creating a usable weapon.

The main challenge? Making the mechanism as compact, reliable, and durable as possible while minimizing the number of components and the overall thickness. I also want to develop a deliberate activation method that reduces the chance of accidental deployment.

Design Choices

As can be seen in my initial sketch, I have taken inspiration from compact retractable tools and sliding mechanisms. I have been adapting these ideas to create a design that is both simple to assemble and strong enough to withstand repeated use. My current concept is theoretically only three thin layers thick: the sliding rail, the central mechanism containing the springs and locking features, and the outer sliding plate. Keeping the design this thin should help minimize its overall size, although this will need to be verified once I begin modeling and prototyping.

Fabrication & Testing

So far, I have developed the initial concept for the flat components, including the rail, locking features, and sliding plate, which I plan to manufacture using waterjet cutting where appropriate. The remaining structural components will hopefully be 3D printed. In theory, assembly should be as simple as stacking the layers together, but I expect there will be challenges with alignment, clearances, friction, and ensuring that the mechanism moves consistently without jamming.

Future Development

My next step is to start CAD modeling the mechanism while taking manufacturability, component clearances, and ease of assembly into account. Once the design is developed further, I hope to fabricate a prototype and test how reliably and consistently it extends and retracts over repeated cycles.

SIDE-PROJECT 02

Electromagnetic Rail Cannon

In Progress

Circuit Design · Systems Integration · Electromagnetics · Electrical Safety

Project Description & Goals

Starting in December 2025, I began exploring electromagnetic propulsion, inspired by an old high-school physics test involving an electromagnetic sliding rail. The project focuses on the electrical circuit design required to store and rapidly discharge energy, converting it into electromagnetic force and mechanical motion. Alongside this, I want to explore the challenges of managing high currents and stored electrical energy safely, while integrating the electrical system with a compact mechanical assembly.

Design Choices

One of my main design choices was to use copper for the conductive rails, with graphite encasing the ends of the sliding copper bar. This combines copper's high electrical conductivity with graphite's conductive and lubricating properties, potentially reducing friction while avoiding direct copper-on-copper contact at the sliding interface.

Another consideration was reducing the risk of contact welding. High currents can cause localized heating at contact points, potentially fusing metallic surfaces together. Since graphite does not form metallic welds in the same way as copper, I wanted to explore its potential as an alternative sliding contact material, while accounting for its own limitations, including overheating, arcing, and wear.

Mechanically, I designed the assembly to be mounted on the forearm, making compactness and the integration of electrical and mechanical components important considerations. This also introduces additional safety challenges that need to be addressed before any practical testing, particularly given the stored electrical energy and intended location of operation.

Fabrication & Testing

I initially planned to 3D print the casing from PLA, but further research raised concerns about its suitability near copper components that could become hot during operation. I am now considering whether a redesigned mount using a more suitable material would be necessary, alongside appropriate electrical isolation and thermal management.

I also have access to the Student Machine Shop, which provides an opportunity to explore the fabrication of the copper and graphite components. A major part of the development process will be understanding how material selection, electrical contact behaviour, and mechanical tolerances affect the reliability of the overall system.

Future Development

As the original design is now somewhat dated, I am considering redesigning the system into a more compact and manufacturable form that avoids the limitations of the original PLA casing. I have also begun researching the PCB and power-electronics side of the project, particularly how energy storage, circuit protection, and controlled discharge should be approached. This will require balancing electrical performance with thermal management, component selection, and safe handling of stored energy.

PROSPECTIVE-PROJECT 01

Ring-Based Computer Mouse

PROSPECTIVE/RESEARCH PHASE

Software Design · Electro-Mechanical Integration · Design/Programming Challenge

Project Description & Goals

Inspired by some recent video's I have been watching of others attempting this project, as well as my annoyance in having to carry my computer mouse with me everywhere, I have started researching into creating a computer mouse that can be mounted into a simple ring to wear on my right pointer finger!

My main goal for this project is to expand my programming and software knowledge, as it is a subject area I would like to increase my proficiency in! Additionally, as a design challenge, I hope to make my design compact and sleek enough that the ring seems like a regular piece of jewlery.

Ideal Design Ideas

Scrolling

For the scrolling of the mouse, I was inspired by the copious amount of spinning fidget rings I own, into trying to create this ring to be a spinning ring that allows you to scroll through webpages by spinning the ring. As I am still in the research phase, I am still looking into the best way of accomplishing this.

Left/Right Clicking

Regarding many other designs I have seen online, I would like to ideally avoid using physical buttons for my mouse buttons. Rather, I want to make the sides of the ring touch sensative, where you can just tap or place your thumb or middle finger against the ring to simulate a left vs right click. This would obviously be difficult to implement underneath the scrolling wheel of the ring, hence why more research must be done.

On/Off and Connectivity

Ideally, I would like to turn the mouse on/off by pressing and holding for 3 seconds on the top of the mouse. Likewise, I would like to put a USB-C charging port on the top of the ring so it can be charged for wireless use through bluetooth ideally. Through the bluetooth, I would like mouse control to be controlled through the movement of the user's hand, i.e. moving your hand up to the right from where the mouse was turned on (home) would move the cursor up and to the right.

Future Development

As this project is still entirely in its research phase, all that is to be done in the upcoming times is further research into viability and how to start this project.

VOLUNTARY COURSE-RELATED PROJECT

4-DOF Servo Robot Arm

Ongoing

Robot Kinematics · Mechanical Design · Servo Control · Arduino · 3D Printing

Project Description & Goals

I am developing a low-cost, servo-driven 4-DOF robotic arm with a gripper, applying the forward, inverse, and velocity kinematics developed in ELEC 442 to a physical manipulator. The goal is to connect theoretical models of robot motion with practical control and manipulation.

Design & Implementation

The proposed design uses four revolute joints actuated by MG996R servos, with 3D-printed links and brackets forming the structure. An Arduino Uno will handle servo control, while a laptop runs the kinematics software. A separate servo power supply is planned to accommodate the actuators' current requirements.

I intend to develop a Denavit–Hartenberg model and implement forward and inverse kinematics to calculate and command gripper poses. The manipulator Jacobian will also be used to investigate velocity control, singular configurations, and Cartesian motion.

Future Development

The main objectives are to demonstrate Cartesian motion and pick-and-place tasks, then evaluate how servo limitations, mechanical tolerances, and calibration affect the accuracy of the kinematic model. As a stretch goal, I would also like to explore throwing a small object toward a target using projectile motion and Jacobian-based velocity calculations.
