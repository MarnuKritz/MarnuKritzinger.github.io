01 - Engineering Process

Objectives and Context

ENPH 253 is UBC Engineering Physics' summer Instrumental Design course, in which teams of 4 take part in an intensive, all-encompasing robotics project working up to a competitive autonomous robotics competition at the end.

Competition Overview

The 2026 competition challenged teams to design, build, and program an autonomous Mars rover capable of navigating an 8 × 8 ft course, including a ramp, while completing as many mission objectives as possible. These mission objectives tested a range of abilities, from mechanical manipulation and structural assembly to autonomous navigation and computer vision. The 5 tasks teams could choose from were as follows:

1 / 2

Mission Objectives

  1. Rock retrieval (2 points): Locate, retrieve, and carry a rock containing aluminum through the course.
  2. Alien identification (1 point per alien): Locate and identify alien figurines (Teletubbies) throughout the course using computer vision, with false positives earning no points.
  3. Radio tower construction (1 point per piece): Assemble a three-piece radio tower from components arranged on the ground into a fully upright structure.
  4. Solar panel recovery (2 points): Locate and uncover a hidden solar panel.
  5. Habitat construction (1 point per piece): Assemble a circular habitat from four separate pieces.

Our team chose to focus on all tasks except the habitat construction, maximizing our potential points at 9

02 - Engineering Process

Engineering Design

As the Mechanical Lead for our team, I was responsible for the design and fabrication of most mechanical components, including the entire chassis, drivetrain, lead screw mechanism, limit-switch integration, absolute-positioning deadwheel systems, and chain-lift system. I also contributed to soldering and assembling protoboard circuits, while taking primary responsibility for electrical integration between systems, including PCB management, wiring connections, and cable routing and management.
