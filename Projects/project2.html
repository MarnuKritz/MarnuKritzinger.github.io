Project 02

Mechanical On/Off Magnet

Originally starting as a subdrone mount for UBC UAS' side VTOL project, I designed a permanent magnet that could be turned on and off through simple rotation. This project involved research, design, iteration, and testing, and is now in its final testing stages.

My role: Designer/Lead

Team: Individual Project

Technical Overview

01 - Engineering Process

Problem & Objectives

In January 2026, I was tasked with working on UAS' side project: A gass-powered Mothership VTOL drone. The goal of this drone was to house/carry a smaller subdrone on the back of the fuselage designed to collect samples or data from natural environments.

the VTOL would then be utilized by companies that needed to collect samples from different ecosystems to fly prolonged distances to these ecosystems, land, release the subdrone to collect samples, then collect the subdrone again and return with the samples.

1 / 2

Objectives and Constraints

The challenge for me? Designing a reliable subdrone mounting system that could funnel the subdrone into place even if the landing wasn't 100% accurate, securely lock it in place during flight, and fit within the confined space of the fuselage.

After extensive research into electro-mechanical switch magnets, magnetic chucks, mechanical landing locks, and other options, as well as consulting my subteam lead, we settled on using magnetic force to hold the drone in place.

This decision ultimately came down to reliability. Mechanical holding systems can have some shaking or wiggle room, potentially allowing components to loosen during flight. A magnetic connection offered a different approach to keeping the subdrone securely attached. From here, I had to design a magnetic system that could maintain a strong connection to the subdrone without requiring electrical power to sustain the connection.
